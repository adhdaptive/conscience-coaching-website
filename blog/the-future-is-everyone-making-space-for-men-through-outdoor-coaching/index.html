The Future Is Everyone: Making Space for Men Through Outdoor Coaching

A reflection on men’s wellbeing, International Men’s Day and the space that outdoor coaching can create.

By Angela Topping · 29 September 2026

  • Outdoor coaching
  • Wellbeing
  • Nature connection

What does it mean to be a man today?

It is a simple question, but perhaps not one we ask often enough.

Being a man can mean many things: being a partner, father, son, friend, colleague, leader, provider, carer or role model — or simply finding your own way through life.

Men can experience pressure to be successful, strong, resilient and dependable while balancing work, relationships, family responsibilities, expectations, and their own ambitions. Sometimes there is little opportunity to stop and consider how they are actually doing.

Through Conscience Coaching and Consulting, I believe that having a place to pause, reflect and think differently can make a real difference to health and wellbeing.

International Men’s Day 2026: The Future Is Everyone

International Men's Day takes place on 19 November 2026, with the theme “The Future Is Everyone.”

The day provides an opportunity to celebrate the positive contributions men make to families, workplaces and communities, while creating conversations around men's health, wellbeing and the challenges men can experience.

It is also an opportunity to ask:

What helps men thrive?

What helps men feel connected, purposeful and well?

Where can men find time to step away from the demands of everyday life and think about the person they want to be?

These conversations don't have to happen in a traditional coaching room.

Sometimes they can happen while walking.

The value of walking and talking

There is something different about having a conversation while walking.

Rather than sitting opposite someone, walking side-by-side can feel more natural and less formal. There is movement, fresh air, changing scenery, and the possibility of comfortable silence.

The conversation can unfold naturally.

It might start with work, relationships, feeling stuck, or wanting to make a change. As the walk continues, there can be space to explore what sits underneath those things:

What really matters to me?

What do I want my life to look like?

What does success mean to me now?

What would I like to change?

Sometimes we simply need the opportunity to slow down enough to hear our own answers.

Reconnecting with nature

Outdoor coaching isn't simply about taking a coaching conversation outside. The environment becomes part of the experience.

Walking through woodland, following a coastal path, moving through open countryside, noticing the seasons, hearing birdsong or simply feeling the weather can help us reconnect with the natural world.

Modern life can make it easy to spend much of our time indoors, looking at screens and moving between work, home and other responsibilities.

Being outdoors gives us an opportunity to reconnect with our surroundings and bring our attention back to the present moment.

The latest People and Nature Survey for England, covering April 2025 to March 2026, found that 89% of adults who had visited a green or natural space in the previous two weeks agreed that spending time outdoors was good for their mental health, while 84% agreed that being in nature makes them very happy.

Nature isn't a cure for mental ill health, nor a substitute for professional treatment when that is needed. But reconnecting with nature can be part of a broader approach to physical and emotional wellbeing.

Moving the body, creating space to think

Walking brings another important element: movement.

Physical activity can support both physical and mental wellbeing, and walking provides an accessible way to build movement into everyday life.

Combining movement, conversation, and nature creates a different experience from sitting indoors.

The body is active.

The senses are engaged.

The environment changes.

And the conversation can move too.

A problem that felt overwhelming can sometimes become more manageable. A decision can become clearer. A goal that seemed distant can begin to feel achievable.

What does it mean to be a man?

International Men's Day also gives us an opportunity to explore what masculinity means today.

Many men grow up with messages such as:

Be strong. Be independent. Work hard. Provide. Be successful. Keep going. Don't show weakness.

Some of these qualities can be valuable. Strength, responsibility, resilience, and ambition all have their place.

But being strong doesn't have to mean doing everything alone.

Being successful doesn't have to be measured entirely through status or achievement.

And taking care of your wellbeing doesn't make you less capable.

Perhaps being a healthy, confident man also includes being able to pause, reflect, understand your emotions, ask for help when needed and make choices that align with your values.

Coaching provides an opportunity to explore these ideas without judgement — not to tell someone what they should think, but to help them think for themselves.

Solution-focused coaching: looking towards what is possible

Through Conscience Coaching and Consulting I use a solution-focused coaching approach in coaching conversations.

This allows us to explore not only the challenges you are experiencing, but also what you want to create.

We might explore:

What would you like to be different?

What is already working?

What strengths do you have?

What is important to you?

What would you notice if things started to improve?

What is one small step you could take?

You don't need to have all the answers before you start.

You don't even need to know exactly what you want to change.

Sometimes the first step is simply creating enough space to think.

Take a walk. Start a conversation

I believe that creating space for reflection can be an important part of a healthy life.

The solution-focused outdoor coaching approach brings coaching into natural environments, creating opportunities to walk, talk, reflect and reconnect.

It might be a coastal walk, woodland trail, park, or open landscape.

Wherever the conversation takes us, the aim is the same:

To create space for you to think about where you are, where you want to go, and what might help you get there.

This International Men's Day, give yourself something that can be surprisingly difficult to find in everyday life - Time.

Time to step away.

Time to breathe.

Time to move.

Time to notice.

Time to think.

And time to consider what comes next.

The future is everyone. Creating a healthier future can start by giving yourself permission to look after your own wellbeing today.

Step outside. Start the conversation. See what becomes possible.
