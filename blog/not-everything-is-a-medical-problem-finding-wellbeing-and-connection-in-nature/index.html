Not Everything Is a Medical Problem: Finding Wellbeing and Connection in Nature

Looking beyond diagnosis and treatment to the wider circumstances that shape our wellbeing.

By Angela Topping · 1 October 2026

  • Wellbeing
  • Nature connection
  • Workplace wellbeing
  • Coaching

What if supporting people to stay in work, or return to work after ill health, was not simply about treating a health condition?

What if we looked at the whole person – their circumstances, relationships, confidence, sense of purpose and belonging, and the environments in which they live and work?

At Conscience Coaching & Consulting, we believe that not everything that affects our wellbeing is a medical problem.

Our wellbeing is shaped by the social, economic, and environmental circumstances around us. Sometimes what someone needs is not another appointment or assessment, but a supportive conversation, connection with others, a sense of purpose and an opportunity to reconnect with themselves and the natural world.

Work and wellbeing are connected

Good work can provide much more than an income. It can bring routine, identity, purpose, social connection and a sense of contribution.

Equally, being away from work because of ill health can affect confidence, relationships, finances and our sense of belonging. Returning to work can feel daunting, particularly when someone is unsure whether they can return to their previous role or working pattern.

These are not necessarily medical problems.

They are often life problems, social problems, and workplace problems.

This is why the WorkWell approach is important. It recognises that supporting people with health-related barriers to employment requires more than healthcare alone, bringing together health, employment and wider community support.

It reflects the social determinants of health – including employment, income, housing, relationships, community connection, and our physical environment.

And this raises an interesting question:

Could nature be part of the answer?

Nature as part of wellbeing

There is growing interest in nature-based social prescribing – supporting people to engage in activities such as walking, gardening, conservation, and community activities in natural environments.

The national evaluation of Green Social Prescribing found statistically significant improvements across the four ONS personal wellbeing measures following participation in nature-based activities. It also identified wider benefits including connection, confidence, meaningful activity, and social relationships.

This doesn't mean that going for a walk is a cure for poor mental health, or that nature replaces medical or therapeutic support.

It means that nature can be one part of a wider approach to wellbeing.

For someone who has been away from work, the first step towards participation may not be returning immediately to their previous role.

It might be getting outside. Meeting someone for a walk. Rebuilding a routine. Having a conversation about what matters.

These may sound like small steps, but they can help people reconnect with themselves, with others and with their communities.

Taking the coaching conversation outdoors

A coaching conversation doesn't have to happen across a desk.

Walking side by side can create space for reflection. Being outdoors can encourage us to slow down, notice, and breathe. The natural environment becomes part of the conversation rather than simply the backdrop.

At Conscience Coaching & Consulting, we offer coaching, wellbeing walks and wellbeing retreats that create opportunities to reconnect – with ourselves, with others and with nature.

For someone navigating a return to work, coaching might explore what a good working life looks like now, what has changed, what gives them energy and what a sustainable return could look like.

The aim isn't simply to get someone “back to work”.

It is to explore what a healthy, meaningful and sustainable way forward could look like for them.

Reconnecting with people – and with place

Many people experience disconnection – from other people, their communities, and the natural world around them.

Wellbeing walks and retreats can create opportunities to meet others, share experiences, and feel part of something.

They also give us an opportunity to notice the places in which we live.

The changing seasons. Trees. Coastlines. Birds. Weather. Light. Water.

When we slow down enough to notice these things, our relationship with our environment can change.

At a time when we need to pay attention to the wellbeing of our planet as well as our own wellbeing, reconnecting with nature feels increasingly important.

Not everything is a medical problem

Perhaps we need to change the question.

Instead of asking only: “What is wrong with this person?”

we could also ask: “What is happening in this person's life, and what might help them move towards the life they want?”

Medical and clinical support can be essential. But not every barrier to wellbeing can be treated with medicine.

Sometimes the barrier is loneliness. Sometimes it is financial insecurity, loss of confidence, a difficult working environment, or lack of purpose.

Sometimes someone simply needs the opportunity to step outside and remember that they are more than their illness or their absence from work.

Making time and space for non-clinical support

If we are serious about helping people stay in work, return to work and live well, we need to think beyond clinical interventions.

Healthcare has an essential role to play, but it cannot address every factor that influences wellbeing. People also need opportunities to reflect, reconnect, and take meaningful steps forward.

For employers, this means considering what support sits alongside occupational health, people services and clinical services.

How can we maintain someone's connection with work before absence becomes long-term?

How can we help rebuild confidence before they return?

How can we create a sustainable return to work, rather than simply focusing on getting someone through the door?

Non-clinical approaches such as coaching, social prescribing, community activity and connection with nature can complement existing workplace and healthcare support.

At Conscience Coaching & Consulting, we are open to work with employers and organisations through coaching, wellbeing walks and wellbeing retreats.

Our approach can support people who are trying to remain connected to work, navigating a period of ill health, preparing to return, or looking for ways to strengthen their wellbeing at work.

We believe employers have an important role to play in creating cultures where asking for support is not a sign of failure, and where wellbeing is understood as more than the absence of illness.

If you are an employer looking for complementary, non-clinical approaches to support wellbeing, retention and sustainable return to work, we would welcome a conversation.

Let's explore what could work for your people, your organisation and your community.

Because not everything is a medical problem.

And sometimes, supporting someone's wellbeing starts with creating the opportunity to connect – with themselves, with other people and with the world around them.
